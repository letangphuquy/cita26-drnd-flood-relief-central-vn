Here is the precise guidance on Sample Average Approximation (SAA) and Out-Of-Sample (OOS) evaluation, tailored directly to your MO-IHLNDP formulation.

### 1. Core Concepts

* **Sample Average Approximation (SAA):** A Monte Carlo-based technique for solving stochastic programs. Instead of evaluating the true expected value over a continuous or massive discrete distribution of scenarios, SAA generates a finite, independent identically distributed (i.i.d.) random sample of $N$ scenarios. The stochastic program is replaced by a deterministic equivalent formulation over this sample. As $N \to \infty$, the optimal solution of the SAA problem converges to the true optimal solution.
* **Out-Of-Sample (OOS):** A validation framework. Because the SAA model optimizes over a limited sample $N$, its objective value is typically biased downward (optimistic). OOS evaluates the *first-stage* decisions generated by the SAA model against a much larger, independent set of scenarios $N'$ to estimate the true expected performance and bounds.

### 2. Variable Classification in Your Model

To apply two-stage stochastic programming techniques, you must partition your variables:

* **First-Stage Variables (Here-and-Now):** Decisions made before the disaster strikes, independent of scenario $s$.
* $x_k$: Planned hub locations.
* $q_k$: Pre-positioned inventory amounts.


* **Second-Stage Variables (Wait-and-See):** Recourse decisions made after a specific scenario $s$ realizes.
* $y_{ks}$: Reactive hub locations.
* $z_{iks}, z_{jks}$: Assignments.
* $f_{khms}$: Lateral trans-shipment flows.


* **Stochastic Parameters ($\xi_s$):** The uncertain data vectors per scenario $s$.
* $\xi_s = (a_{uvms}, D_{is}, O_{js}, F_{ks}^a, \tau_{ks}, r_{us}, \lambda_{is}, c_{jks}, \Theta_{kis})$



### 3. Application Guide to Your MO-IHLNDP Model

Because your model is Multi-Objective ($Z_1, Z_2$), you will typically apply an $\epsilon$-constraint or weighted sum method to handle the objectives. The SAA/OOS procedure below assumes you are evaluating a specific scalarized objective or finding a point on the Pareto front.

#### Step 1: In-Sample Optimization (SAA)

1. **Generate Sample:** Draw an i.i.d. sample of $N$ scenarios $\mathcal{S}_N = \{\xi_1, \xi_2, \dots, \xi_N\}$ from the true distribution. Assign equal probabilities: $\pi_s = 1/N \quad \forall s \in \mathcal{S}_N$.
2. **Solve SAA:** Solve your full deterministic equivalent MILP (Constraints 4 - 17) using only the set $\mathcal{S}_N$.
* Minimize $\hat{Z}_1^N, \hat{Z}_2^N$.


3. **Extract Decisions:** Store the optimal first-stage variables. Let's call them $\hat{x}_k$ and $\hat{q}_k$.

#### Step 2: Out-Of-Sample Evaluation (OOS)

1. **Generate Test Set:** Draw a new, massive i.i.d. sample $\mathcal{S}_{N'}$ (e.g., $N' = 10,000$), completely independent of $\mathcal{S}_N$.
2. **Fix First-Stage Variables:** In your mathematical model, set $x_k = \hat{x}_k$ and $q_k = \hat{q}_k$ as constants.
3. **Solve Second-Stage per Scenario:** Because the first-stage variables are fixed, the large model decomposes. You do not need to solve a monolithic model for $N'$ scenarios. Instead, solve a separate, much smaller recourse problem for *each* scenario $s' \in \mathcal{S}_{N'}$:

$$\text{Min } Z_{1, s'} = \text{Reactive Setup}(s') + \text{Origin to Hub}(s') + \text{Inter-hub}(s') + \text{Last-Mile}(s')$$



Subject to constraints (4) - (17) formulated only for the specific scenario $s'$, treating $\hat{x}_k$ and $\hat{q}_k$ as fixed parameters.
4. **Compute OOS Metrics:** Calculate the true expected performance of your first-stage design:

$$Z_{1}^{OOS}(\hat{x}, \hat{q}) = \sum_{k \in \mathcal{H}} (F_k \hat{x}_k + c_k \hat{q}_k) + \frac{1}{N'} \sum_{s'=1}^{N'} Z_{1, s'}$$



Repeat the equivalent calculation for $Z_2^{OOS}$.

#### Step 3: Gap Analysis

Compare the In-Sample objective $\hat{Z}_1^N$ with the OOS objective $Z_1^{OOS}$.

* If $Z_1^{OOS} \approx \hat{Z}_1^N$, your sample size $N$ is sufficient, and the network design ($\hat{x}, \hat{q}$) is robust against the uncertainties.
* If $Z_1^{OOS} \gg \hat{Z}_1^N$, the model suffers from the "optimizer's curse" (overfitting to $\mathcal{S}_N$). You must increase $N$ in Step 1 and repeat the process.
